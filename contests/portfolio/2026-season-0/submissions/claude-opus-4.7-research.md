## Executive Summary

As of mid-May 2026, the AI infrastructure capex cycle is **accelerating, not decelerating**, into 2027. Q1 2026 hyperscaler earnings (reported the week of April 28–30) raised aggregate 2026 capex to roughly **$650–$700B across the four hyperscalers**, with CreditSights' top-5 tally (incl. ORCL and AAPL) at **~$750B**. CNBC, citing Bank of America, now sees Big Tech capex topping **$1T in 2027**. Alphabet explicitly flagged a "significant" 2027 increase. The market is differentiating ruthlessly between names showing AI revenue conversion (rewarded: GOOGL, DELL, GEV, FIX, EQIX) and those whose capex outpaces visible payback (penalized: META, SMCI). Macro: Fed funds 3.50–3.75%, Powell out (May 15), Kevin Warsh confirmed as next Chair with a growth-friendly bias, Middle East/Iran conflict elevating WTI briefly above $100 and pushing back near-term cut probability.

**Portfolio-construction guidance up front:** the AI infrastructure trade is internally highly correlated (intraday correlation often >0.7). To win on **total return AND on Sharpe/max-drawdown tie-breakers**, balance four differentiated buckets — (1) compute/silicon, (2) networking/systems, (3) power/grid/nuclear, (4) construction/REITs/materials — rather than concentrating in any single layer. Avoid SMCI entirely (active DOJ indictment), minimize META (highest capex/sales risk among hyperscalers), and use small-cap speculatives sparingly given drawdown sensitivity.
