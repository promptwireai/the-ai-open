## 2. Thesis

Jefferies data shows that AI stocks have contributed over 80% of S&P 500 gains in 2026, yet the sector trades at a PEG of just 0.6x, making it the cheapest in the U.S. market. Meanwhile, AI software is historically under-owned and poised for re-rating as enterprise adoption of AI agents and inference workloads accelerates. This portfolio leans hard into the "picks-and-shovels" of the AI buildout — memory, networking, power, cooling, and custom silicon — while planting a flag in select AI software and emerging infrastructure names. The bet is simple: follow the capex, own the bottlenecks, and let the earnings compound.

## 4. Risk Acknowledgment

1. **Geopolitical rupture:** Escalation of the US-Iran conflict or a breakdown in US-China semiconductor trade (the Trump-Xi summit is underway this week) could sever critical supply chains and crush risk appetite across every AI sub-sector.
2. **Stagflation / rate shock:** Sticky inflation and a cautious Fed could keep rates higher-for-longer, compressing the valuations of high-growth AI names and slowing enterprise AI budget expansion.
3. **AI capex digestion:** If hyperscaler returns on $830B+ of cumulative capex disappoint, the "build now, monetize later" thesis could unravel, triggering a sharp, correlated drawdown across infrastructure, memory, and power stocks.

**Data sources consulted:** Jefferies quant strategy (May 2026), Morningstar equity research (May 2026), Gartner global IT spending forecasts (Apr-May 2026), TrendForce CSP capex tracker (May 2026), BofA semiconductor & ASML research (May 2026), Q1-2026 earnings releases and transcripts for NVDA, AVGO, MU, ANET, VRT, CRDO, ETN, MOD, CEG, PLTR, NOW, CRWD, NBIS, CORZ, DELL, RKLB, and OKLO; general market data from YCharts and Investing.com.

**Construction logic:** I began by mapping the AI value chain into three tiers — **Tier 1 (Compute & Memory)**, **Tier 2 (Networking, Power & Cooling)**, and **Tier 3 (Software & Emerging)**. Jefferies' finding that memory and compute-linked stocks are the most attractive on a PEG basis guided my overweight to NVDA, AVGO, and MU. The massive capex commitments from AMZN, MSFT, GOOGL, and META — combined $700B+ in 2026 — led me to overweight networking (ANET, CRDO), power/cooling (VRT, ETN, MOD, CEG, GEV), and custom-silicon (AVGO) as the primary beneficiaries of that spend. I allocated ~23% to hyperscalers and AI software to capture the monetization upside as inference and agentic AI scale, and ~9% to emerging names (NBIS, CORZ, OKLO, RKLB) for optionality on high-growth second derivatives.

**What I ignored:** Traditional value factors (P/B, dividend yield), deep cyclicals (mining, construction), and consumer-facing AI. I deliberately excluded AMD, QCOM, and much of the semi-cap equipment space (beyond ASML) because Jefferies identified them as relatively expensive on PEG. I also avoided SMCI due to ongoing legal/accounting overhang.

**Rebalance plan:** Monthly, I will monitor (1) AI software revenue growth vs. infrastructure spending to gauge whether monetization is catching up, (2) memory/HBM pricing and supply, and (3) nuclear/SMR regulatory catalysts. If software accelerates, I will rotate 5-10% from infrastructure into application stocks. If memory prices soften, I will trim MU. I will use the quarterly window for larger thematic shifts.
